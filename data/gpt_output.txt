The prior call gives a useful baseline for language. Management and analysts used “inflation” repeatedly, and the CFO used *“deflationary”* and *“deflation”* in very specific, technical contexts:

- “headwinds from **deflation in dairy**”
- “Egg **deflation** was a meaningful headwind…”
- “the **deflationary** impacts from eggs”

So they are comfortable and precise with this word when it’s a real driver of comps, but they are not throwing around macro terms loosely—everything is framed in terms of “food inflation,” “headwinds,” “price investments,” etc.

For the upcoming Q2:

- Current macro commentary around Kroger (Reuters piece, analyst notes, etc.) stresses *rising inflation* again and a “challenging grocery backdrop,” not broad-based food deflation.
- The recent Q1 call guidance explicitly: “we expect **inflationary pressure to increase** as the year progresses, reflecting the broader macro environment.”
- The current narrative is a *price war* and cost-saving–funded “affordability” investments, not that Kroger is being hurt by general deflation. Any deflation that matters is narrow (e.g., specific categories like dairy or eggs).

That combination makes it more likely that:

- They’ll continue to discuss “inflation” (and maybe “disinflation” / “lower inflation”), and
- Use **category-specific** language like “deflation in eggs/dairy” if those remain large enough headwinds.

The key question is whether the *exact word* “deflation” will appear at least once, either in prepared remarks or in Q&A.

Evidence in favor:
- It was used multiple times last quarter, cleanly, by the CFO. That sets precedent and vocabulary.
- Eggs/dairy deflation were material in Q1; if category price declines continue to impact comps, they may again be described as “deflation” or “deflationary.”

Evidence against:
- Management has guided to *higher* inflation as the year progresses; if that played out, the narrative might pivot away from deflation as a key factor.
- Even if some categories are still down year-on-year, they might summarize as “lower inflation” or “lapping price declines” rather than explicitly saying “deflation.”
- The word is somewhat technical; its use hinges on whether they want to emphasize a very specific comp headwind again. If egg/dairy price pressure has normalized, they may simply not need the term.
